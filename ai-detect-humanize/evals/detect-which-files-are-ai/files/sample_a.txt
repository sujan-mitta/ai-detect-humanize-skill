Whales are large marine mammals belonging to the order Cetacea, which also includes dolphins and porpoises. They are fully aquatic, breathe air through blowholes on the top of their heads, and give birth to live young that are nourished with milk. Cetaceans are traditionally divided into two groups: baleen whales, which filter small organisms such as krill and plankton from the water using plates of keratin called baleen, and toothed whales, which hunt fish, squid, and other animals using teeth and echolocation. The blue whale is the largest animal known to have existed, reaching lengths of more than 30 meters.

Whales are found in oceans throughout the world, and many species undertake long seasonal migrations between feeding grounds in polar and temperate waters and breeding areas in warmer regions. Their social structures vary considerably, from the small, stable pods of killer whales to the more loosely organized groups of many baleen species. Many whales communicate using a range of sounds, including the complex songs produced by male humpback whales, which can be heard over considerable distances.

Whales were heavily exploited by commercial whaling during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, which reduced many populations to a small fraction of their original size. International protections introduced in the late twentieth century have allowed some populations to recover, although whales continue to face threats from ship strikes, entanglement in fishing gear, pollution, noise, and climate-related changes to their prey and habitats.
