City Council Approves New Bike Lane Network

The Riverside City Council voted 7-2 on Tuesday evening to approve a $14 million plan that will add 38 miles of protected bike lanes across the city over the next four years. The decision marks a significant milestone in the city's efforts to promote sustainable transportation and improve road safety for all residents.

The plan, which was developed over 18 months in consultation with community groups, local businesses, and transportation experts, prioritizes connections between residential neighborhoods, schools, and the downtown business district. Construction on the first phase, covering the Elm Street and Harbor Avenue corridors, is expected to begin in the spring.

"This is a historic day for Riverside," said Councilmember Dana Ortiz, who championed the proposal. "These bike lanes will make our streets safer, reduce traffic congestion, and help us meet our climate goals. It's a win for everyone."

However, the plan has not been without its critics. Some business owners along Harbor Avenue have expressed concerns about the loss of on-street parking, which they fear could impact customer access. Councilmember Raymond Hale, who voted against the measure, argued that the city should focus on repairing existing roads before investing in new infrastructure.

City officials say they will work closely with affected businesses to minimize disruption and explore alternative parking solutions. Additionally, the plan includes funding for public education campaigns to promote safe cycling and driving practices.

As cities across the country continue to rethink their transportation networks, Riverside's investment reflects a growing recognition that safe, accessible cycling infrastructure is essential for building healthier, more livable communities.
